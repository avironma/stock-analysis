Guide / README

v6.0 — Resilience & whole-portfolio planning. The new tab lets you enter non-equity asset allocations, configure optional asset-class / jurisdiction / single-stock guardrails, view four qualitative stress scenarios, and map holdings by inferred portfolio function. Scenario outputs are heuristic screening indicators based on static attributes, not forecasts or probabilities. Listing-region concentration is only a proxy; it does not replace revenue, domicile and supply-chain analysis.

This dashboard is designed to answer two different questions separately: Which businesses rank highest? and Which combination should actually be owned? The first is handled in the ranking tabs; the second in Portfolio Lab.

Recommended workflow: start with the Global Top 20, adjust the Scoring Model only if you want to change the investment philosophy, review company cards, then build and stress-test a portfolio in Portfolio Lab.

1Explore the Global Top 20

Use Region to switch between Global, Europe, North America and Asia-Pacific. Use Rank by to sort by Composite, Quality, Investment, Moat, Resilience or Portfolio Fit.

The ranking is dynamic. If you change metric weights in the Scoring Model tab, this table reranks automatically.

2Use filters deliberately

The search box matches company, ticker, country, region, sector, driver and role. Seat-test only removes watchlist names. Show controls whether you see the Top 10, Top 20, Top 30 or the full universe.

3Understand the scores

Quality measures business durability. Investment reflects valuation-adjusted attractiveness. Portfolio Fit asks whether the company improves the portfolio. Replaceability measures scarcity. Resilience captures robustness across economic regimes.

Composite is the final weighted score using your chosen decision weights.

4Adjust the investment philosophy

Open Scoring Model to change the importance of moat, financial quality, cash-flow predictability, adaptability, management, governance and anti-fragility.

You can also change how much the final ranking depends on Quality, Investment, Fit, Replaceability and Resilience.

Each weight group is normalized automatically in the calculations, but keeping the visible total near 100% makes interpretation easier.

5Read the company cards

Click any company name in the ranking to jump to its card. Cards show the investment thesis, score summary, valuation snapshot and main risks.

Use this to understand why a company ranks where it does rather than relying on the number alone.

6Build the portfolio

Add names individually from the ranking, or use Load shown Top 20 to portfolio. In Portfolio Lab you can edit every weight with sliders or numeric inputs.

Presets provide different starting points: Balanced Global, Max Quality, Defensive, Europe Only, US Tilt and Asia-Pacific Tilt.

7Interpret portfolio diagnostics

Composite and Quality are weighted portfolio averages. Effective holdings falls as concentration rises. Region and economic-driver charts reveal hidden clustering that sector labels may miss.

Warnings flag excessive cash, concentration or regional exposure.

8Know what is static

Market data is a static snapshot dated 15 August 2026. P/E ratios, market caps and the Investment score do not update automatically.

Business-quality factor scores are intended to change much more slowly and should only be revisited after meaningful company developments.

Important interpretation rules

  • A high-quality business can still rank lower if valuation is unattractive.
  • A high-ranked company does not automatically belong in the portfolio if it duplicates an existing economic driver.
  • The Global Top 20 is a ranking, not a buy list.
  • Portfolio diversification should be assessed by economic drivers, geography and resilience—not sector labels alone.
  • Refresh valuation-sensitive inputs before acting on the dashboard after significant market moves or company news.

Guide / README

This dashboard is designed to explore roughly 100 long-term compounder candidates using one consistent framework, then build a portfolio that balances quality, valuation, resilience and diversification.

Quick start

  1. Start in Global Top 20. Choose a region and ranking method, then use the thematic/risk filters to define the opportunity set you actually want.
  2. Browse the full 100-company universe. The Universe tab is intentionally broad so you can discover alternatives beyond the current Top 20. Click any company name to open its card.
  3. Tune the model. In Scoring Model, change how much importance you assign to moat, financial quality, adaptability, management and other factors.
  4. Build a portfolio. Add companies from the ranking or universe, or load one of the preset portfolios.
  5. Adjust position sizes. Use the sliders or number fields in Portfolio Lab.
  6. Check diagnostics. Review concentration, region exposure, economic-driver exposure, quality and resilience.

Reviewed vs screening-stage companies

Committee reviewed companies have been through the earlier investment-committee process. Screening companies were added to broaden the opportunity set and have preliminary factor and investment scores. They are useful for exploration but should be promoted to committee-reviewed status before relying on them for a final investment decision.

Prefer vs Require vs Exclude

Prefer adds a modest ranking bonus without removing alternatives. Require is a hard screen. Exclude removes companies that match that characteristic. This is useful for expressing a philosophy such as “prefer electrification exposure, require a strong balance sheet, but do not require a dividend.”

Equal-weight allocation

Click Equal-weight portfolio to allocate 100% equally across all companies currently in the Portfolio Lab. For example, six holdings are allocated at approximately 16.7% each. The dashboard still applies your portfolio guardrails afterwards, so an equal-weight portfolio with fewer than five holdings may trigger the default 20% single-stock warning.

Balance sheet / leverage

v5.6 adds an explicit indebtedness factor for all 100 companies. For ordinary corporates the primary measure is Net debt / EBITDA. Net cash receives the strongest score; leverage above 3× is penalised increasingly. The default model assigns 12% of the Quality Score to Balance Sheet / Leverage.

Portfolio analysis

After adding holdings and setting weights, click Analyze portfolio. The tool evaluates the portfolio as a whole: quality, resilience, concentration, region mix, economic-driver overlap, risk clusters and what type of next holding would improve diversification.

Portfolio guardrails

The Portfolio Lab can now flag excessive single-stock, regional, high-AI, China, cyclicality and defence exposure. The default single-stock limit is 20%. Guardrails are diagnostics rather than an optimizer: they warn you but do not automatically change position sizes.

Using thematic and risk filters

Filters change the opportunity set; they do not secretly alter company scores. For example, selecting AI exposure → Exclude high removes high-AI-exposure companies from the displayed ranking, while leaving the scoring model itself unchanged. This keeps personal preferences separate from the analytical score.

Understanding the ranking

The ranking separates three different questions:

  • Is this a great business? Answered mainly by the Quality Score.
  • Is this attractive at today's valuation? Reflected in the Investment Score.
  • Does this improve the portfolio? Reflected in Portfolio Fit, Replaceability and Resilience.

The Composite Score combines these dimensions using your chosen decision weights. A company can therefore be a superb business but rank lower if valuation is unattractive or if it overlaps too much with existing portfolio exposures.

How the portfolio tools behave

  • Add inserts a company into the portfolio at a small starter weight.
  • Normalize rescales all current holdings so the invested total becomes approximately 100%.
  • Cash / unallocated is simply 100% minus the total invested weight.
  • Effective holdings falls as the portfolio becomes more concentrated, even if the number of holdings stays unchanged.
  • Region exposure groups holdings by Europe, North America and Asia-Pacific.
  • Economic-driver exposure groups holdings by the underlying source of returns, which can reveal hidden overlap better than sector labels alone.

Using the presets

The presets are not recommendations. They are starting configurations for experimentation:

  • Balanced Global spreads exposure broadly across regions and business models.
  • Max Quality emphasizes companies with the highest current business-quality scores.
  • Defensive emphasizes resilience.
  • Europe Only / US Tilt / Asia-Pacific Tilt help test geographical preferences.

How to interpret changes

If changing one model weight causes large ranking changes, that means the companies are relatively close and your investment philosophy matters materially. If rankings barely move, the result is more robust to model assumptions.

A useful workflow is to test several philosophies—for example moat-heavy, valuation-heavy and resilience-heavy—and look for companies that remain near the top across all of them.

Seat Test

The Seat Test asks:

If there were only one portfolio seat left, does this company deserve it over every remaining alternative?

This prevents the portfolio from filling up with merely good businesses. The final portfolio should contain companies that are both individually strong and collectively complementary.

iPhone / iPad usage

The dashboard is designed to run in Safari on iPhone and iPad. For the most reliable experience, open it from a normal web URL (for example GitHub Pages, Netlify or Cloudflare Pages). When opened directly as a downloaded file:// document, iOS may restrict persistent browser storage. The dashboard will still run, but saved settings may not persist between sessions.

Reading valuation data

The dashboard distinguishes reported TTM P/E from the preferred screening P/E. Normally they are the same. Where GAAP trailing earnings are temporarily distorted, the preferred value can use a verified forward multiple instead. A status label tells you when that happens. This prevents a maximum-P/E filter from treating accounting anomalies as genuine valuation signals.

Data and refresh policy

The dashboard is a static snapshot, not a live terminal. Market-sensitive inputs such as P/E, market cap and Investment Score are dated 15 August 2026. They should be refreshed after large price moves, earnings releases, acquisitions, major regulatory developments or material changes in management or strategy.

The company-quality factors change more slowly and should only be updated when the underlying business case changes.

What this tool is not

This is not an optimizer that predicts returns, and a high score is not a guarantee of performance. The purpose is to make assumptions explicit, compare companies consistently and expose concentration or overlap that may otherwise be missed.
